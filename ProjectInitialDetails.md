---

## 1. Problem Statement and Target Users

### What real-world problem does your application aim to solve?
* Not all eateries provide a clear ingredient list for their dishes. Our application aids users in detecting possible ingredients specific to their dietary restrictions which they are not able to consume.
* Different or complex local hawker dishes may have non-obvious hidden ingredients or cross-contamination (e.g., lard, shrimp paste, peanut oil).

### Who are the intended users of the application?
* Individuals who may be unaware of the ingredients of a specific dish.
* Individuals who have food allergies.
* Individuals with dietary restrictions due to religious beliefs or lifestyle choices (e.g., no pork, vegetarian).

---

## 2. User Inputs
Users provide the following information via the command-line interface (CLI):
* User's specific dietary restrictions (allergies, religious constraints, etc.).
* Name of the dish that the user is planning to eat.
* Location, stall name, or Hawker Centre.

---

## 3. Use of AI

### How will AI be utilized within the application?
* Variable inputs provided by the user are sent to a Large Language Model (LLM) via API.
* It identifies possible food restrictions based on the users’ food profile (allergic reactions, religious constraints).
* The AI acts as a comprehensive reasoning engine to uncover hidden ingredients and possible cross-contamination based on general knowledge and customer reviews.

### What outputs, insights, or recommendations will the AI generate?
* Highlight possible allergens present that may affect the user.
* Highlight ingredients that may be contradictory to the user’s religious guidelines.
* Identify cross-contamination risks, such as shared cooking equipment or preparation spaces.
* Output an estimated risk level (Low, Medium, or High) indicating if ingredients violate the user's restrictions.
* Provide a short explanation or reasoning for the outputs.

---

## 4. Business Rules

### Business Rules
* The AI must check against a standardized allergen list from the allergen database reference (e.g., the “Big 9” allergens: milk, eggs, peanuts, tree nuts, soy, wheat, fish, shellfish, sesame). Allergen lists must be up-to-date and accurate.
* All matching allergies or dietary restrictions must be included in the output.
* The AI must include a safety disclaimer: *Always remind users that the AI is advisory and not a substitute for professional medical advice. Please seek opinions from store owners for clarification on ingredients.*

### Validations
* The AI has to prioritize specified dietary restrictions based on religious or lifestyle constraints.
* All required outputs (correct data types, etc.) must be validated before proceeding.
* Inputs provided by the user must be clear such that the AI can identify the dish and flag food items correctly. Ingredient recognition and cross-contamination must be validated in the response.

### Decision-Making Logic
* Reasoning and output must state whether the dish may or may not be safe to eat based on the risk score generated by the AI.
* Program logic categorizes risk scores into thresholds (e.g., risk score 70% = High Risk).
* The AI validates if foods are commonly cross-contaminated (e.g., oats may contain gluten).
